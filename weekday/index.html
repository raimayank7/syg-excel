WEEKDAY function in Excel

WEEKDAY returns the day of the week of a date as a number.

WEEKDAY tells you which day of the week a date falls on, as a number. With it you can mark weekends, total sales by day of the week, find the Monday of any week, or check whether a holiday lands on a Sunday.

Syntax

=WEEKDAY(serial_number, [return_type])
ArgumentWhat it is
serial_numberA date, or a cell that holds one.
return_typeWhich day counts as 1. Left out or 1: Sunday is 1 and Saturday is 7. With 2: Monday is 1 and Sunday is 7. With 3: Monday is 0 and Sunday is 6.

Examples

Each result below is what Excel itself showed for the formula on the sample sheet.

  • =WEEKDAY(D2)
    Result: 2

    26 January 2026 with the default numbering, where Sunday is 1. Monday is 2.

  • =WEEKDAY(D2,2)
    Result: 1

    The same date with return type 2, where Monday is 1.

  • =TEXT(D2,"dddd")
    Result: Monday

    For the name of the day, TEXT is simpler.

  • =WEEKDAY(D4,2)
    Result: 6

    15 August 2026 with Monday as 1: a 6 means Saturday.

  • =IF(WEEKDAY(D4,2)>5,"Weekend","Working day")
    Result: Weekend

    With Monday as 1, anything above 5 is Saturday or Sunday.

  • =D4-WEEKDAY(D4,2)+1
    Result: 10-Aug-2026

    The Monday of the week that contains the date.

  • =D4+8-WEEKDAY(D4,2)
    Result: 17-Aug-2026

    The next Monday after the date.

  • =SUMPRODUCT((WEEKDAY(D2:D5,2)>5)*1)
    Result: 1

    How many of the four holidays fall on a weekend.

The numbering

The second argument decides which day is number 1. It is worth setting it every time, because the default surprises people.

return_typeSundayMondayTuesdayWednesdayThursdayFridaySaturday
1 or left out1234567
27123456
36012345

Return type 2 is the most convenient. Monday to Friday are 1 to 5, and the weekend is 6 and 7, so "is it a weekend?" becomes simply WEEKDAY(date,2)>5.

Types 11 to 17 start the week on other days: 11 is the same as 2, 12 starts on Tuesday, and so on up to 17, which starts on Sunday.

The name of the day

WEEKDAY gives a number. For a name, TEXT is the direct route: =TEXT(A2,"dddd") gives Monday, and "ddd" gives Mon. Or simply format the date cell itself with the custom format dddd.

Marking weekends

  • A label: =IF(WEEKDAY(A2,2)>5,"Weekend","Working day")
  • Shading rows: in Conditional Formatting, use the formula rule =WEEKDAY($A2,2)>5.
  • Where only Sunday is a holiday: =WEEKDAY(A2,2)=7.

Totals by day of the week

Add a helper column with =WEEKDAY(A2,2) or =TEXT(A2,"ddd"), and total by it with SUMIF or a PivotTable. Shops use this to see which days are busiest. Without a helper column, SUMPRODUCT does it: =SUMPRODUCT((WEEKDAY(A2:A13,2)=6)*F2:F13) adds up Saturday sales.

Moving to a particular day

With return type 2, the weekday number is also "how many days into the week".

To getFormula
Monday of the same week=A2-WEEKDAY(A2,2)+1
Sunday at the end of the week=A2-WEEKDAY(A2,2)+7
The following Monday=A2+8-WEEKDAY(A2,2)
The previous Friday=A2-WEEKDAY(A2,2)-2

Grouping dates by "week starting" uses the first of these as a helper column.

Working days

To count working days between two dates, or to find the date a number of working days ahead, do not build it from WEEKDAY. NETWORKDAYS and WORKDAY are made for that, and they also accept a list of holidays.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming Monday is 1 without giving the second argument.
  • #VALUE! when the date is stored as text.
  • An empty cell returns 7 with the default numbering, because day zero in Excel's calendar is treated as a Saturday. Blank rows can therefore be counted as Saturdays. Guard with IF(A2="","",...).
  • A result shown as a date in January 1900 because of leftover date formatting. Set the cell to General.

In Google Sheets

WEEKDAY is the same in Google Sheets, with return types 1, 2 and 3.
