MOD function in Excel

MOD returns the remainder after one number is divided by another.

Divide 20 by 12 and you get 1 with 8 left over. MOD gives you the 8. A remainder sounds like a small thing, but it is the key to anything that repeats in a cycle: every second row, every third item, days of the week, hours on a clock.

Syntax

=MOD(number, divisor)
ArgumentWhat it is
numberThe number to divide.
divisorThe number to divide by.

Examples

Each result below is what Excel itself showed for the formula on the sample sheet.

  • =MOD(E2,12)
    Result: 8

    20 units divided into dozens leaves a remainder of 8.

  • =MOD(E3,2)
    Result: 0

    Remainder on dividing by 2: 0 for an even number, 1 for an odd one. 50 is even.

  • =IF(MOD(E9,2)=0,"Even","Odd")
    Result: Odd

    The usual even-or-odd test. E9 holds 3.

  • =MOD(ROW(A5),2)
    Result: 1

    ROW gives the row number, 5. The remainder shows whether a row is odd or even, the basis of striped shading.

  • =MOD(135,60)
    Result: 15

    Minutes left after taking out the full hours from 135 minutes.

  • =MOD(-7,3)
    Result: 2

    With a negative number the result takes the sign of the divisor: 2, not -1.

  • =MOD(10,0)
    Result: #DIV/0!

    Dividing by zero is not possible: #DIV/0!.

How it works

MOD divides the number by the divisor and returns what is left after taking out as many whole divisors as possible. The result always lies between 0 and the divisor. When the number divides exactly, the result is 0.

Uses

Even or odd. =MOD(A1,2) is 0 for even numbers and 1 for odd numbers.

Every nth row. =MOD(ROW(),3)=0 is TRUE on every third row. Used as a formula rule in Conditional Formatting, =MOD(ROW(),2)=0 shades alternate rows. Inside SUMPRODUCT it adds every other row.

Hours and minutes. =INT(135/60) gives the hours and =MOD(135,60) the minutes left. The same pattern splits paise from rupees, inches from feet and items from full boxes.

The time part of a date and time. =MOD(A1,1) is the fraction of a day, the time, whatever the date. It is shorter than A1-INT(A1).

Shifts that cross midnight. With a start time in A1 and an end time in B1, =MOD(B1-A1,1) gives the right duration even when the shift ends after midnight, where a plain subtraction would be negative.

Does it divide exactly? =MOD(A1,5)=0 tests whether A1 is a multiple of 5. A leap year test is built from three such checks.

Going round in a loop. To number a long list 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3, write =MOD(ROW()-2,3)+1 starting in row 2. This shares people into teams or tasks into a rota.

Negative numbers

The result of MOD always carries the sign of the divisor. =MOD(-7,3) is 2, not -1. Excel takes out whole threes going downwards, which reaches -9, and 2 is what must be added to get back to -7. Many programming languages give -1 here. If your sheet has negative values, test what you get.

Common mistakes

  • #DIV/0! when the divisor is zero or an empty cell.
  • Tiny decimal errors. =MOD(1.1,0.1) may not be exactly 0. Work in whole numbers where you can, for example in paise.
  • Confusing MOD with a percentage. In some programming languages the percent sign means remainder. In Excel, % after a number means "divided by 100".

In Google Sheets
