Where to start

New to formulas? Read these two first, in order.

  1. How to write a formula in Excel: the equals sign, operators, the order of calculation, functions and copying down.
  2. Relative and absolute cell references: what the dollar sign does, and when you need it. More wrong answers come from a missing dollar sign than from any other cause.

Something has gone wrong? Excel formula errors and how to fix them goes through #N/A, #VALUE!, #REF!, #DIV/0!, #NAME? and the rest, with the formula that produces each and its cure.

Lookups

Fetching a value from another table is the task people most often need help with.

  • INDEX and MATCH together, step by step builds the pair slowly, then shows lookups to the left, two-way lookups and lookups on two conditions.
  • VLOOKUP or XLOOKUP puts the two side by side on the same data and helps you decide which to use in your office.

Everyday tasks

  • Percentage formulas: percent of a total, percentage change, adding and removing a percentage, and the mistake of taking tax off the wrong figure.
  • Date formulas: ages, days between dates, due dates, month ends, working days and the financial year.
  • Cleaning and splitting text: spaces, capitals, splitting names and codes, and turning text into real numbers.

Excel and other programs

  • Excel and Google Sheets: where formulas differ, for anyone who moves files between the two.

How to get the most from a guide

Open Excel beside the page. Type the small sample sheet into a blank workbook, or use data of your own with the same shape. Then type each formula yourself. Copying and pasting is quicker, but typing a formula once, and seeing it fail when a bracket is missing, teaches more than reading it ten times.

When a formula works, change it. Point it at a different cell. Remove a dollar sign and copy it down to see what breaks. Formulas are learnt by experiment.
