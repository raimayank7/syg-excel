SEQUENCE function in Excel

SEQUENCE returns a list of numbers in order, such as 1 to 10, in rows and columns of your choosing.

SEQUENCE makes numbered lists: 1, 2, 3, or 10, 20, 30, or a run of dates. It replaces typing two numbers and dragging the fill handle, and because it is a formula, the list can change length by itself. It is available in Excel 2021 and Microsoft 365.

Syntax

=SEQUENCE(rows, [columns], [start], [step])
ArgumentWhat it is
rowsHow many rows of numbers to make.
columnsHow many columns. 1 if left out.
startThe first number. 1 if left out.
stepHow much to add each time. 1 if left out.

Examples

Each result below is what Excel itself showed for the formula on the sample sheet.

  • =SEQUENCE(5)
    Result:
    1
    2
    3
    4
    5

    The numbers 1 to 5, one under another.

  • =SEQUENCE(1,5)
    Result:
    12345

    One row and five columns: the same numbers across.

  • =SEQUENCE(3,4)
    Result:
    1234
    5678
    9101112

    Three rows of four: the numbers fill along each row, then move down.

  • =SEQUENCE(5,1,10,10)
    Result:
    10
    20
    30
    40
    50

    Five numbers starting at 10 and going up in tens.

  • =SEQUENCE(4,1,100,-25)
    Result:
    100
    75
    50
    25

    A negative step counts down.

  • =SEQUENCE(COUNTA(B2:B8))
    Result:
    1
    2
    3
    4
    5
    6
    7

    Serial numbers for a list: as many as there are items, growing as items are added.

  • =SEQUENCE(6,1,DATE(2026,11,1),7)
    Result:
    Sun 01-Nov-2026
    Sun 08-Nov-2026
    Sun 15-Nov-2026
    Sun 22-Nov-2026
    Sun 29-Nov-2026
    Sun 06-Dec-2026

    Result:
    Apr-2026
    May-2026
    Jun-2026
    Jul-2026

    The first day of four months in a row: SEQUENCE supplies 0, 1, 2 and 3 months to add.

  • =LARGE(D2:D8,SEQUENCE(3))
    Result:
    1299
    650
    450

    The three highest prices, by handing LARGE the positions 1, 2 and 3.

How it works

The first argument says how many rows of numbers you want. The others are optional: how many columns, where to start, and what to add each time. The numbers spill from the formula's cell.

With more than one column, the numbers run along the first row, then the second, as when reading a page.

Serial numbers that keep themselves right

A serial-number column typed by hand has to be fixed whenever a row is added or deleted. =SEQUENCE(COUNTA(B2:B100)) produces exactly as many numbers as there are entries. Add an item and a number appears beside it.

Dates

A date is a number, so SEQUENCE makes dates too. Format the cells as dates.

  • Every day of a month: =SEQUENCE(30, 1, DATE(2026,11,1))
  • Every Monday for ten weeks: =SEQUENCE(10, 1, DATE(2026,11,2), 7)
  • A calendar grid: =SEQUENCE(6, 7, start_date) gives six weeks of seven days.

Months cannot be made with a fixed step, since they differ in length. Hand SEQUENCE to EDATE instead, as the eighth example does: =EDATE(start, SEQUENCE(12,1,0)) gives twelve month starts. This is the quick way to lay out an EMI or rent schedule.

Feeding other functions

Many functions take a position number: the nth largest, the nth character, the nth row. Give them SEQUENCE and they return all the positions at once.

  • =LARGE(range, SEQUENCE(5)) is the top five.
  • =INDEX(SORT(range), SEQUENCE(10)) is the first ten rows of a sorted list.
  • =MID(A1, SEQUENCE(LEN(A1)), 1) splits a text into single characters.

Tables of results

Because SEQUENCE can run across as well as down, two of them make a grid. =SEQUENCE(10)*SEQUENCE(1,10) is a ten by ten multiplication table from a single formula: a column of 1 to 10 multiplied by a row of 1 to 10.

Repeating patterns

With MOD, a sequence becomes a cycle. =MOD(SEQUENCE(12)-1,3)+1 gives 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3 and so on, which assigns twelve people to three teams in turn.

Counting down, and decimals

A negative step counts downwards. The step can be a fraction too: =SEQUENCE(5,1,0,0.25) gives 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.

Before Excel 2021

Older versions have no SEQUENCE. The usual substitutes are =ROW()-1 copied down a column, which gives 1, 2, 3 from row 2, or =ROWS($1:1), which is not upset when rows are inserted above.

Common mistakes

  • #SPILL! when cells in the way are not empty.
  • #NAME? in Excel 2019 and earlier.
  • #CALC! when the number of rows works out to zero, for example when counting an empty list.
  • Confusing the order of the arguments: rows, columns, start, step. To get a single column starting at 100, the columns argument still needs its 1: =SEQUENCE(5,1,100).
  • Dates showing as five-digit numbers until the cells are formatted.

In Google Sheets

SEQUENCE is available in Google Sheets with the same four arguments.
