Financial functions

PMT function in Excel

PMT works out the fixed payment for a loan from the interest rate, the number of payments and the amount borrowed.

PMT answers the question everyone asks before borrowing: "what will the instalment be?" Given the loan amount, the interest rate and the number of payments, it returns the fixed amount to be paid each period. It is the formula behind an EMI on a reducing balance.

This page explains the arithmetic of the function. It is not financial advice. A real loan may add fees, insurance and taxes, and lenders round and schedule payments in their own ways, so the figure on a loan offer can differ a little. Take the lender's own statement as the figure that counts.

Syntax

=PMT(rate, nper, pv, [fv], [type])
ArgumentWhat it is
rateThe interest rate for one payment period. For monthly payments, the yearly rate divided by 12.
nperThe total number of payments. For monthly payments, years times 12.
pvThe present value: the amount borrowed.
fvThe balance wanted after the last payment. 0 if left out, which is right for a loan that is paid off.
type0 or left out if payments are made at the end of each period, 1 if at the beginning.

Examples

Each result below is what Excel itself showed for the formula on the sample sheet.

  • =PMT(B2/12,B3*12,B1)
    Result: -10379.18

    The monthly instalment on 5,00,000 at 9 percent a year for 5 years. It is negative because it is money you pay out.

  • =-PMT(B2/12,B3*12,B1)
    Result: 10379.18

    A minus sign in front shows it as a positive figure.

  • =ROUND(-PMT(B2/12,B3*12,B1),0)
    Result: 10379

    Rounded to the nearest rupee.

  • =-PMT(B2/12,B3*12,B1)*B3*12
    Result: 622750.66

    The total paid over the five years: the instalment times 60.

  • =-PMT(B2/12,B3*12,B1)*B3*12-B1
    Result: 122750.66

    The total interest: everything paid, less the amount borrowed.

  • =-PMT(B2/12,10*12,B1)
    Result: 6333.79

    The same loan over 10 years: a smaller instalment.

  • =-PMT(B2/12,10*12,B1)*120-B1
    Result: 260054.64

    But much more interest in total.

  • =-PMT(B2,B3,B1)
    Result: 128546.23

    Yearly payments: the yearly rate and the number of years, with no dividing or multiplying.

The three figures, in matching units

The one rule that matters: the rate and the number of payments must be for the same period.

Payments areratenper
monthlyyearly rate / 12years * 12
quarterlyyearly rate / 4years * 4
yearlyyearly rateyears

Using the yearly rate with a count of months is the classic mistake. It produces an absurdly large instalment.

Enter the rate as a percentage or a decimal: 9% or 0.09. Typing 9 means nine hundred percent.

Why the answer is negative

Excel's financial functions follow the direction of the money. The loan arrives in your pocket, so it is positive. The instalments leave it, so they are negative. Put a minus sign in front of PMT, or in front of the loan amount, to see a positive figure.

What the instalment is made of

Each instalment pays some interest and some of the amount borrowed. Early on, most of it is interest. As the balance falls, more goes towards the loan itself. Two companion functions split any single instalment:

  • =IPMT(rate, period, nper, pv) is the interest part of the instalment numbered period;
  • =PPMT(rate, period, nper, pv) is the part that repays the loan.

The two always add up to PMT. A repayment schedule lists the period numbers down a column, with IPMT, PPMT and the remaining balance beside each.

Comparing options

The last examples compare five years with ten. A longer term lowers the instalment and raises the total interest, often by a great deal. A small table with terms down the side and PMT beside each, plus the total paid, makes the trade-off plain. Put the rate and amount in cells, not in the formula, so that you can change them and watch the whole table respond.

Working backwards

The same family of functions solves for any one unknown.

To findUse
the instalmentPMT
how much you can borrow for a given instalmentPV
how many payments it will takeNPER
the interest rate implied by an offerRATE
what regular savings will grow toFV

Saving towards a target is PMT with the future value filled in: =-PMT(rate/12, months, 0, target) is the amount to set aside each month to reach the target, at an assumed steady rate of return.

Common mistakes

  • A yearly rate with monthly payments, or the reverse.
  • The rate typed as 9 instead of 9%.
  • Surprise at the negative sign.
  • #NUM! when a figure is impossible, such as a negative number of payments.
  • Treating the result as the full cost of a loan, ignoring charges that PMT knows nothing about.

In Google Sheets

PMT is the same in Google Sheets, as are IPMT, PPMT, PV, FV, NPER and RATE.
