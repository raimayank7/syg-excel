COUNTIF function in Excel

COUNTIF counts the cells in a range that meet one condition.

COUNTIF answers "how many?" with a condition attached: how many students passed, how many orders are pending, how many times a name appears. Along with SUMIF, it is the function most used to summarise a list.

Syntax

=COUNTIF(range, criteria)
ArgumentWhat it is
rangeThe cells to look at.
criteriaThe condition: a value, a comparison in quotation marks, or a cell reference.

Examples

Each result below is what Excel itself showed for the formula on the sample sheet.

  • =COUNTIF(B2:B9,"8A")
    Result: 4

    How many students are in class 8A.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,">=75")
    Result: 4

    How many Maths marks are 75 or more.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,"<40")
    Result: 1

    How many are below 40.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,78)
    Result: 2

    How many times the exact mark 78 appears.

  • =COUNTIF(A2:A9,"*a")
    Result: 4

    A wildcard: names that end with the letter a.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,">"&AVERAGE(C2:C9))
    Result: 5

    A comparison with a calculated value: how many marks are above the average.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:E2,">=80")
    Result: 2

    Along a row: in how many subjects Asha scored 80 or more.

  • =COUNTIF(C2:C9,C2)>1
    Result: TRUE

    A duplicate test: is the value in C2 found more than once in the column?

How it works

COUNTIF looks at each cell of the range, tests it against the condition and counts the cells that pass. It has only two arguments, so there is nothing to line up.

cells that are not empty"<>"

Two rules cover most mistakes. A comparison sign goes inside quotation marks. A cell reference stays outside them and is joined on with &.

Text matching ignores capital and small letters.

Finding duplicates

COUNTIF is the standard tool for spotting repeated values.

  • In a helper column, =COUNTIF($A$2:$A$100,A2) shows how many times each entry occurs. Anything above 1 is repeated.
  • =COUNTIF($A$2:A2,A2) with a range that grows as it is copied down numbers each occurrence: 1 for the first, 2 for the second. Filtering for values above 1 shows only the extra copies.
  • As a Conditional Formatting rule, =COUNTIF($A$2:$A$100,A2)>1 colours every duplicate.

Is this value in that list?

=COUNTIF(list, A2)>0 is TRUE when A2 appears in the list. Put it beside one list to check each entry against another. It is the simplest way to compare two lists.

A percentage

Divide by the total count for a share: =COUNTIF(C2:C9,">=33")/COUNT(C2:C9) is the pass rate. Format the cell as a percentage.

Counting within bands

For "from 40 up to 59" you need two conditions, which is COUNTIFS. With COUNTIF alone, subtract: =COUNTIF(C2:C9,">=40")-COUNTIF(C2:C9,">=60").

Limits and surprises

  • Long numbers. COUNTIF compares only the first 15 digits of a number, so two different 16-digit card or account numbers stored as text can count as equal.
  • One condition. For more, use COUNTIFS.
  • Calculations on the range are not allowed. =COUNTIF(MONTH(A2:A13),3) is not accepted, because the first argument must be a real range. Use SUMPRODUCT for that.
  • Closed workbooks. A COUNTIF that points to another workbook shows #VALUE! when that workbook is closed.

In Google Sheets

COUNTIF is the same in Google Sheets.
